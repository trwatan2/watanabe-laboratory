3|We connect molecular transformations to processes through catalysts, reaction environments, energy supply and material circulation.
4|Creating reaction environments with electricity.
5|We supply power directly to catalyst structures to deliver heat where it is needed. For high-temperature reactions such as dry reforming, we study catalytic reactions together with heat transport.
6|Efficient local heating · Reaction-environment control · Energy utilization
7|From CO₂ to reusable carbon.
8|We develop reaction systems that convert CO₂ into syngas and solid carbon for carbon circulation. By combining reaction equilibrium, carbon deposition, separation and recovery, we investigate CO₂ conversion at the reactor scale.
9|Syngas production · Solid-carbon recovery · Carbon circulation
10|Turning catalyst geometry into reaction performance.
11|We build reactors using spiral structures, porous metals and washcoats, considering fluid flow and heat and mass transfer alongside catalyst materials. Visualizing structure–flow interactions helps us actively control the reaction environment.
12|Enhanced flow · Improved heat transfer · Higher space velocities
13|Changing chemical processes through heat delivery.
14|We investigate processes that introduce electrical energy directly into reactors and catalysts. Rapid response, local heating and compact equipment enabled by electrification support new modes of reaction operation.
15|Direct heating · Rapid response · On-site reaction processes
16|Using lattice sulfur as a reactant in catalysis.
17|Sulfur in sulfide lattices acts as a reactive species that takes up hydrogen from hydrocarbons and leaves as H₂S. Sulfur from H₂S is then reinserted into the resulting vacancies, enabling redox cycles that repeatedly use lattice sulfur.
18|We control exchangeable surface lattice sulfur, vacancy formation and regeneration, and metal–sulfur interfaces to explore dehydrogenation and new catalytic processes involving sulfur compounds.
19|Our approach connects materials chemistry with reaction engineering, structured catalysts and energy supply.

4|About Watanabe Laboratory
5|Based at Shizuoka University’s Hamamatsu Campus, our laboratory studies catalysis and reaction engineering involving CO₂, CH₄, hydrogen, carbon resources and sulfur species.
6|Beyond evaluating catalyst activity, we combine structured catalysts, fluid flow, heat and mass transfer, and electrical energy to develop research that can be applied to practical reactors and processes.
7|We examine each reaction through four perspectives: chemistry, structure, energy and circulation.
8|Understand reaction pathways through catalyst surfaces, lattice species and redox behavior.
9|Build reaction environments using spirals, porous structures and washcoats.
10|Supply electricity directly to catalysts and reactors to heat the required locations.
11|Cycle CO₂, solid carbon and sulfur species to keep using them as resources.
12|Selected externally funded research projects.
